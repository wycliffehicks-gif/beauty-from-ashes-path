## What changes

An invitation should give the person one concrete anchor and one linked reflective
focus grounded in the day's teaching and actual selections. The anchor might be
one ordinary moment, detail, word, situation, chosen quality or observable sign.
The focus explores one aspect of meaning, context, difference or a personally
chosen direction. Specificity must help the reader begin; it must not invent the
reader's history, feelings, needs or preferred change.

One coherent activity is the rule, not a rigid limit of one question mark. A short
concrete setup followed by one linked exploration can be one activity. It should
not expand into an interview, several questions or a list of assignments. Writing
always needs a clear sentence opening and one distinct **after-writing** direction
that returns to the person's own words with a specific aspect to consider. Merely
finishing the sentence, or a generic request to explain its meaning, is insufficient.
Quiet reflection or meditation likewise needs a clear focus and instruction.
No unsupplied breathing routine or body scan is added.

Carl's example about a time when hope was clearer and what differed then versus
now illustrates a concrete comparison. It is not a universal memory question or
wording to repeat. A readily available memory can fit, but the candidate must not
assume prior hope, safety or support, require painful recollection, or imply that
the present is worse. A concrete present detail or potential observable sign can
fit instead. A sign of desired change requires support for that direction; a
blank selection does not provide it. No exercise requires a positive ending.

A label containing A or B establishes neither both feelings nor both conditions.
If another selection separately confirms B, accurately acknowledging B does not
establish A. Tentative phrasing does not excuse an unsupported addition. Blank or
null stays unknown. A broader concrete detail from the supplied teaching can
support private reflection without probing a subject the person declined.
Explicit no-question or no-exercise choices take precedence; acknowledgment
alone remains available.
